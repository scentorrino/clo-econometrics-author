### 1.3 G-1 — CLOSED. What the full text shows

**Poirier (2017), *Efficient estimation in models with independence restrictions*, JoE 196(1), 1–22** — `poirier2017`. The narrow question was: **does any example put an endogenous regressor inside the nonparametric component, making that example's nuisance problem ill-posed?**

**The answer is no.** From a direct read of the full text:

- **Example 1.2 (Instrumental Variables Regression):** $Y = X'\theta_0 + U$ with $U \perp Z$. Poirier's own words: *"This is a **linear IV model** with independent errors."* Fully parametric — no nonparametric component at all. **This resolves the ambiguity that kept the gate open:** "instrumental variables regression model" in the abstract means **linear IV**, not NPIV.
- **Example 1.5 (Partially Linear Regression):** $Y = X\theta_0 + G_0(W,\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon \perp (X,W)$ — the nonparametric component's argument $W$ is **exogenous by assumption**. Poirier describes it as *"a generalization of the Robinson (1988) partially linear regression model which allows the marginal effect of $W$ on $Y$ to vary across observationally equivalent units,"* attributing the generalization to **Santos (2011)**. It reduces to $\rho(Y,X,\theta) \perp X \mid W$.
- **Monte Carlo (§4):** a Card (1999) returns-to-schooling design with a binary instrument and endogenous $X$ = years of schooling, but $Y = X\theta_0 + U$ — again fully linear.

**On the round-1 confabulation.** The words *"ill-posed inverse problem"* and *"Tikhonov regularization"* do appear in the paper, which explains where the fabricated output came from — but the context is the opposite of what was reported. In §1.2, discussing CGMM: *"This CGMM (Continuum of GMM) estimator will be efficient when the optimal weighting operator is used… More generally, this problem suffers from the ill-posed inverse problem, and a Tikhonov regularization is suggested. **By comparison, the estimator we propose uses a finite but growing number of zero-covariance conditions**…"* He raises ill-posedness only to explain how he **avoids** it, and it concerns the **weighting operator**, not a structural nuisance.

**Provenance note.** The published version remains paywalled to this agent (ScienceDirect 403); the above is verified against the **working-paper PDF**, whose metadata title matches the published title exactly and whose 22 pages match JoE 196(1), 1–22.

**Round-2 postmortem, for the record.** The round-2 structural inference — that Poirier's class is well-posed, and that optimally-weighted GMM over a growing basis is not how one estimates in an ill-posed problem — is **confirmed correct against the actual text**. What was wrong was the *sourcing*: five quotations attributed to unnamed "published secondary descriptions" that turned out to be web-search summaries, plus a Google Drive download that could not be reproduced. Right conclusion, unsupported evidence. The lesson is recorded rather than the relief.

### 1.4 The strongest single piece of novelty evidence in this file — and it is Poirier's own

Poirier's §5 conclusion, verbatim:

> *"Several extensions of this paper may be interesting. The first would be to generalize our results to models with unconditional independence restriction **with nuisance functions of the type $\rho(Y, X, \theta, h(\cdot)) \perp Z$, where $h(\cdot)$ is a nonparametric object**."*

**The nearest competitor names this paper's setting as an open problem he does not undertake.** That is worth more than any absence-of-evidence argument assembled from citation sweeps, and it belongs in the introduction.

**One honest caveat, to be stated rather than elided.** Poirier writes *unconditional* independence, whereas this paper now maintains the *conditional* form $\Delta U \perp W \mid \Delta Z$ (plan §17). It is therefore the closest available statement of the gap, not a literal description of what we do. The manuscript should quote it and then say precisely that — something like: *"Poirier (2017, §5) identifies models with independence restrictions and a nonparametric nuisance $h(\cdot)$ as the natural next step; we take it up in the conditional-independence form, where the nuisance additionally solves an ill-posed nonlinear operator equation."*

## 2. Suggested contribution statement

> We study the partially linear panel model $Y_{it}=\varphi_\dagger(X_{it})+Z_{it}'\theta_\dagger+\eta_i+U_{it}$, $t\in\{1,2\}$, in which $X_{it}$ is a continuous, scalar, endogenous regressor and the only instrument $W$ is binary. First differencing removes $\eta_i$ and leaves $\Delta Y=\varphi_\dagger(X_{2})-\varphi_\dagger(X_{1})+\Delta Z'\theta_\dagger+\Delta U$. Identification rests on a distributional restriction, $\Delta U\perp W\mid\Delta Z$, together with $E[\Delta U\mid\Delta Z]=0$. Because $W$ is binary, mean independence carries no information about $\varphi_\dagger$; the distributional restriction does, but it makes $\varphi_\dagger$ the solution of an ill-posed **nonlinear** integral equation rather than the linear one familiar from nonparametric instrumental regression and from the first-differenced panel analysis of Fève and Florens (2014). Poirier (2017) computes efficiency bounds under independence restrictions for finite-dimensional parameters and identifies models with a nonparametric nuisance under such restrictions as an open extension; this paper takes up that extension in the conditional form, where the nuisance additionally solves an ill-posed nonlinear operator equation. Our contribution has four components.
>
> **First, a negative result.** No weight measurable with respect to $(W,\Delta Z)$ delivers a moment condition free of $\varphi$ for every admissible $\varphi$ unless the tilted marginal distributions of $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$ coincide — a condition that fails whenever the endogenous regressor drifts over time, as it does in our application. The Robinson (1988) partialling-out argument underlying the partially linear instrumental-variables literature is therefore unavailable. This is not a limitation of our estimator; it is a property of the differenced design.
>
> **Second, a Neyman-orthogonal moment and its source condition.** We combine the mean restriction with a weighted version of the distributional restriction and show that the moment's Gateaux derivative in $\varphi$ vanishes in every direction if and only if the weight solves the adjoint equation $\mathcal{A}^\ast\ell=-\nu$, where $\mathcal{A}$ is the Fréchet derivative of the nonlinear operator and $\nu$ is built from the *difference* of the two period-specific conditional-mean-times-density functions. $\sqrt n$-estimability of $\theta_\dagger$ then requires $\nu\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{A}^\ast)$ — the Severini–Tripathi (2006, 2012) range condition, transplanted onto the Fréchet derivative of a nonlinear operator. When it fails, $\theta_\dagger$ is not $\sqrt n$-estimable, and we report the slower rate and identification-robust intervals rather than concealing the failure.
>
> **Third, a device that keeps the conditioning variables out of the nonparametric machinery.** Because independence is imposed conditionally on $\Delta Z$, exploiting it directly would require smoothing in a three-to-five-dimensional covariate. We instead convert the conditional restriction into a continuum of unconditional moments by a Bierens (1982, 1990) weight family applied to a bounded transform of $\Delta Z$ — equivalently, by a characteristic kernel, the two being linked by Bochner's theorem (Sriperumbudur et al., 2010). Stating the requirement at the level of generically comprehensively revealing families (Stinchcombe and White, 1998) makes the choice of weight a modelling decision rather than a technical accident. The efficient weighting of the resulting continuum is governed by Carrasco and Florens (2000); like Poirier (2017), we resolve the ill-posedness of the optimal weighting operator with a finite but growing basis rather than by regularising the continuum, and we follow Domínguez and Lobato (2004) in treating the choice of family as an identification question rather than a power question.
>
> **Fourth, a computable construction.** We solve the orthogonality condition by imposing it **in sample** — a balancing problem in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with a single ridge parameter, whose bias is bounded by the RKHS norm of an empirical mean embedding — rather than by inverting the population adjoint. We document why the latter fails: the kernel representation of $\mathcal{A}^\ast$ approximates the *continuum* adjoint, so driving its residual to zero leaves the finite-sample bias entirely uncancelled. Cross-fitting removes the empirical-process term, which matters because the regularised inverse with a data-driven stopping rule belongs to no Donsker class.
>
> We give the semiparametric efficiency bound, verify the theory in Monte Carlo, and revisit Chay and Greenstone's (2005) estimates of the capitalisation of particulate pollution into house prices across 989 U.S. counties, where the Clean Air Act's nonattainment designation supplies the binary instrument. Relaxing linearity in pollution changes the picture.

## 3. The nearest competitors, and exactly where each falls short

### Competitor 1 — Florens, Johannes & Van Bellegem (2012), *Econometrics Journal* 15(2), 304–324
Proximity **1** · COMPETITOR. Tikhonov regularisation of the linear ill-posed equation; $\sqrt n$ normality of $\hat\theta$. **Falls short:** conditional-mean restriction ⇒ **linear, fixed** operator whose adjoint is estimable once; ours is a Fréchet derivative at the unknown truth. No panel; continuous instrument, so the projection route is open to them; no conditioning variable, hence no Bierens device.

**One-liner:** *"Florens, Johannes and Van Bellegem (2012) obtain $\sqrt n$ inference on $\theta$ when the nuisance solves a linear ill-posed equation; with a binary instrument the equation is nonlinear, the adjoint depends on the unknown structural function, and the projection argument that partials out $\varphi$ is unavailable."*

### Competitor 2 — Florens & Lapenta (2024), *TEST* 33(3), 897–920
Proximity **1** · COMPETITOR. Same model by **Landweber–Fridman**, avoiding smoothing on the instruments. **Falls short:** the operator is again **linear**; "no smoothing on the instruments" is a *fixed-bandwidth* device, not a conditional-to-unconditional conversion with a consistency guarantee. No panel, no orthogonalisation, no cross-fitting.

**One-liner:** *"Florens and Lapenta (2024) apply Landweber–Fridman to a partly linear model with a linear conditional-mean operator and keep the instrument out of the smoothing by fixing its bandwidth. We apply it to a nonlinear operator generated by an independence restriction, keep the conditioning variables out of the smoothing by a Bierens weight family — exact rather than approximate — and obtain $\sqrt n$ inference through a Neyman-orthogonal moment rather than a projection."*

### Competitor 3 — Chen & Pouzo (2012, 2015), *Econometrica* 80(1) and 83(3), 1013–1079
Proximity **2** · FRAMEWORK. They cover $E[\rho\mid V]=0$ with $\rho$ **nonsmooth and nonlinear in $h$**, including nonlinear NPIV. Under the amended A1 our model is of this form.

**Why it is not a corollary.** (1) Their residual is finite-dimensional; ours is a continuum in $u$ and $t$ — and by **Domínguez & Lobato (2004)** an arbitrarily chosen finite family need not be equivalent to the conditional restriction at all. (2) PSMD requires a nonparametric estimate of $E[\cdot\mid\Delta Z]$ with $\dim\Delta Z=3$–$5$; the Bierens device removes exactly that. (3) Their $\theta$-asymptotics need stochastic equicontinuity over the sieve; an LF iterate with a data-driven stopping rule is not Donsker. (4) No negative result, no panel.

**One-liner:** *"Chen and Pouzo's penalised sieve framework accommodates nonlinear and nonsmooth conditional moment models in principle. Instantiating it here would require a continuum of generalized residuals, nonparametric smoothing in a three-to-five dimensional conditioning variable, and a Donsker property for a regularised inverse with a data-driven stopping rule. We avoid all three."*

### Competitor 4 — Fève & Florens (2014), *Journal of Econometrics* 181(2), 151–164
Proximity **1** · COMPETITOR (panel axis). Already in `instrumental.bib` as `feve2014`.

**Panel data models by first differences** with **endogenous variables** under an **instrumental variables condition**; the framework leads to **linear inverse problems** solved by **Tikhonov regularisation** with an $L^2$ or Sobolev penalty. By **two of the three companion-paper authors**, in the exact referee circle the positioning names, and the closest prior work on the *panel* axis.

**Falls short:** the inverse problem is **linear**; no parametric component at $\sqrt n$; no discrete-instrument requirement; no orthogonalisation; no conditional-to-unconditional device.

The *difference* structure is shared machinery inherited from the differenced panel design; what is new here is that the operator is a **Fréchet derivative of a nonlinear map** rather than a fixed linear conditional-expectation operator.
> 2. **Their non-identification condition is the direct analogue of `rem:nophifree`.** They observe that if the law of $(Z_1,W)$ equals that of $(Z_2,W)$ then $K\varphi=0$ for *every* $\varphi$ and the model is unidentified. That is exactly Remark R2's condition — no $\varphi$-free moment unless the tilted marginals of the two periods coincide — arrived at independently in the linear setting. **Cite it there; it converts R2 from a device into a recognised feature of the differenced design.**
> 3. **Explicit singular system.** $K^\ast K\varphi_j=(\lambda_{2j}-\lambda_{1j})^2\varphi_j$, identified iff $\lambda_{2j}\neq\lambda_{1j}$ — a concrete precedent for H1's $\sigma_j\asymp j^{-a}$ in a differenced panel.

> **Lead for the paper's most exposed flank (A1b(iii), the missing primitive).** Their **Proposition 2.2** gives identification when $(Z_1,Z_2)$ is *strongly identified* by $W$ and $Z_1,Z_2$ are *measurably separated* — a completeness-type primitive for exactly the differenced structure this paper needs. **They immediately add that "the assumption of Proposition 2.2 is too strong,"** which is itself useful: it tells us the honest primitive is hard even in the linear case, and it gives a citable precedent for recording the gap rather than closing it cheaply. **Route to the theorist for A1b(iii).**

**One-liner:** *"Fève and Florens (2014) analyse first-differenced panel models with endogenous variables as a linear inverse problem solved by Tikhonov regularisation. With a binary instrument the mean restriction is uninformative, the inverse problem becomes nonlinear, and a parametric component can no longer be recovered by regularised inversion alone."*

### Competitor 5 — Poirier (2017), *Journal of Econometrics* 196(1), 1–22 — **VERIFIED, and closer than round 1 implied**
Proximity **1** · COMPETITOR (independence axis). Already in `instrumental.bib` as `poirier2017`.

Computes the semiparametric efficiency bound for **finite-dimensional parameters** under **unconditional and conditional independence restrictions**, and gives a consistent, asymptotically normal, efficient estimator built on **a finite but growing number of zero-covariance conditions** that are asymptotically equivalent to the independence restriction. ## 4. How to frame the contribution

### Lead with

1. **Poirier's open-extension sentence (§1.4).** The nearest competitor naming this paper's setting as future work is the single most efficient novelty argument available. Quote it, then state the conditional-vs-unconditional difference honestly.
2. **The impossibility result (Remark R2).** Short, surprising to anyone trained on Robinson, and it establishes necessity in one paragraph.
3. **The transplant of the source condition.** Severini–Tripathi's $\nu\in\mathcal{R}(T^\ast)$ is famous; stating that the same condition governs $\sqrt n$-estimability when $T$ is replaced by the Fréchet derivative of a nonlinear operator is a claim any theorist will want to check. Frame A2 in Escanciano's (2022) regular/irregular language.
4. **Empirical balancing rather than adjoint inversion, with the numerical evidence.** Plan §15's finding — kernel-adjoint discrepancy driven to $0.0000$ with the bias entirely uncancelled, CG diverging $0.06\to 130$ — is a citable methodological lesson. Do not bury it.
5. **One kernel, two jobs** — *a convenience, not a contribution.* See below.

### Two by-products of the Poirier read

**(a) A precedent for plan §14's open weighting question.** Poirier faced the identical design choice — a continuum of moments whose optimal weighting operator is ill-posed — and resolved it with a **finite but growing basis** ($K$, $L$ over polynomials, cubic splines, Fourier series) rather than Tikhonov on the continuum. That is a concrete, citable alternative to inventing a rule, and it sits alongside the Carrasco–Florens route. His conclusion also flags choosing $K$ and $L$ via **higher-order MSE expansions**, citing Donald, Imbens & Newey, as unfinished business — so if the paper wants a principled rule, that is the lead. See Area I in the annotated bibliography.

**(b) A precedent for the A1 discussion.** Poirier has a section headed *"Conditional independence restrictions and partially linear regression example"* showing that *"imposing a conditional moment restriction instead of conditional independence restriction results in a loss of identification."* Cross-reference it at Lemma L1 / Remark R1 — **but state the direction difference.** In Poirier, conditional independence *buys* identification a conditional mean restriction does not. Here, the conditional-independence restriction is *uninformative* about $\theta_\dagger$ (Lemma L1), identification comes from the mean restriction, and independence supplies the debiasing channel. Related, not the same, and the manuscript should say so.

Round 1 claimed the bridge "Bierens weight family $\equiv$ characteristic kernel $\equiv$ injective mean embedding" had not been stated in an econometrics journal. **Withdrawn.** Both obvious sources contain it: **Stinchcombe & White (1998)**, whose GCR theory is the injectivity property for Banach-valued families; and the **Bochner route in Sriperumbudur et al.
